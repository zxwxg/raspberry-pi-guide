    Money-saving rules

    • Never buy a cheap unbranded microSD. A $4 card can cost you a weekend of data.
    • Always buy the official power supply — random chargers cause random crashes that look like hardware faults.
    • A Pi 4 is often enough. Only pay for a Pi 5 if you need 4K, PCIe or speed.
    • Buy 4 GB, not 2 GB, if the price difference is small. RAM is not upgradable.
    • Used cases, HATs and PSUs are usually fine. Used SD cards and used boards with "unknown history" are not.
    • Order the micro-HDMI cable and cooler with the board — separate shipping doubles the real cost.

    6 · The real comparison

    Raspberry Pi vs renting cloud servers

    Nobody is telling you the Pi is always better. Here is the honest arithmetic.

    What you needRenting (typical)Owning a PiBreak-even
    Small always-on server (4 GB RAM) $20–$30 per month = $288/yr once + $12/yr electricity ~9 months
    2 TB of file/photo storage ~$120 per year (commercial cloud storage) once (Pi + 1 TB SSD, add a second disk later) ~2 years
    Home automation hub Vendor hubs: $60–$200 + subscriptions once, no subscription, works offline Immediate if you avoid a subscription
    Retro gaming console Mini consoles: $80–$120, fixed games with two controllers, unlimited systems Comparable — but far more capable
    Development/staging environment $15–$50 monthly per instance once, run 5 containers at home ~6 months
    Uptime and reliability ✓ datacentre SLA, redundant power and network ✗ your power, your internet, your responsibility Cloud wins — always
    Public IP, DDoS protection, scaling ✓ built in Behind Cloudflare Tunnel / WireGuard, or use a cheap relay Cloud wins for big public traffic

    Choose a Pi when…

    • You want to own the hardware and the data
    • The workload is light and constant (DNS, automation, files, small APIs)
    • Downtime matters to you but not to a business
    • You are learning — the cost of breaking it is zero
    • You need GPIO, cameras or physical control

    Rent a server when…

    • Something must be online for customers 99.9 % of the time
    • You need a public IP, TLS certificates and DDoS protection at scale
    • Traffic is spiky and unpredictable
    • Compliance requires professional hosting and backups
    • You need to scale horizontally on demand
    🏆

    The combination most people end up with: a Raspberry Pi at home for automation, files, DNS, media and experiments, plus a small paid VPS only for the things that must never go down. Total cost is often lower than either option alone — and you learn both worlds.

    7 · Don't get burned

    Buying safely, in eight rules

    1. Use the official reseller list

      raspberrypi.com/resellers lists approved distributors per country. This is the only way to be sure of genuine boards, warranty and support.

    2. Assume marketplace listings are fake until proven otherwise

      Counterfeit boards, "Pi 5 compatible" power supplies that deliver 3 A instead of 5 A, and microSD cards with fake capacity are extremely common.

    3. Check the capacity of any SD card you buy

      Tools like H2testw or F3 write real data and verify it. A "512 GB" card that only stores 32 GB will destroy your files silently.

    4. Compare total delivered price, not sticker price

      Shipping, customs and duty can add 30 % when buying from abroad. Local resellers are frequently cheaper overall even at a higher list price.

    1. Buy the power supply and cooler with the board

      They are the two items people skip, and the two that cause the most "my Pi is broken" reports. $17 total on a Pi 5.

    2. Prefer NVMe over microSD for anything permanent

      An M.2 HAT+ plus a 500 GB NVMe costs about $57 and turns a fragile SD-card toy into a reliable server.

    3. Buy used carefully

      Safe to buy used: cases, HATs, cables, official PSUs, keyboards. Risky: microSD cards, heavily used boards (check for bent pins, burn marks, missing capacitors), and anything without photos of the actual item.

    4. Budget for the second purchase

      Almost nobody stops at one board. Plan your first purchase knowing a Zero 2 W at $15 is the natural second device for Pi-hole or a camera.

    Know your total before you order

    Use the calculator above, check the official resellers in your country, and start with one honest project. That is all it takes.
