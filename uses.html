Twelve real uses

How a Raspberry Pi helps us — in practice

Forget the marketing. Here are twelve things people actually run on a Raspberry Pi, with the parts you need, the honest total cost, the difficulty, and what you get out of it. Pick one, do it, then pick another. That is how a $15 board turns into skills, saved money and a more useful house.

🎓 1 · Learn Linux, Python & networking

This is the original purpose of the Pi and still the most valuable one. You get a machine you can destroy and rebuild without consequences, and every command you learn (SSH, permissions, systemd, cron, Docker, nginx) is exactly the same on a $10,000 server in a data centre.

What you need: Pi 4 or Pi 5, PSU, 32 GB microSD, any monitor/TV, keyboard & mouse.

Typical total: · Difficulty: 1/5 · Time: forever, in a good way

First month plan:

  • Week 1 — install Raspberry Pi OS, learn the terminal, files, permissions
  • Week 2 — enable SSH, set a static IP, log in from your laptop
  • Week 3 — write Python scripts, schedule them with cron
  • Week 4 — install Docker and run your first container

Why it helps: these are employable, transferable skills — and the board makes them cheap enough to practise.

🏠 2 · Automate your home with Home Assistant

Home Assistant turns the Pi into the brain of your house. It speaks to thousands of devices — Wi-Fi plugs, Zigbee sensors, Philips Hue, Xiaomi, Sonoff, air conditioners via IR, cameras, doorbells — and puts them all on one dashboard with automations and voice control.

What you need: Pi 5 (4 GB is fine), PSU, 64 GB SD or SSD, a Zigbee USB stick if you want battery sensors.

Typical total: plus devices · Difficulty: 2/5 · Time: one evening

Automations people actually build:

  • "If nobody is home and it is after sunset, turn off every light and set the AC to eco."
  • "If the bedroom temperature drops below 16 °C, start the heater."
  • "Send me a phone notification with a photo when the front door camera sees a person."
  • "Warn me before the water tank or gas cylinder runs empty."

Everything runs locally: it keeps working when the internet or the vendor's cloud goes down, and your habits stay in your house.

☁️ 3 · Your own personal cloud (Nextcloud)

Nextcloud gives you Google-Drive-style file sync, photo backup from your phone, calendars, contacts, notes, shared folders and even document editing — on hardware you own, for one payment instead of a monthly bill.

What you need: Pi 5 · 4 GB or 8 GB, NVMe SSD via M.2 HAT+ (or a USB 3.0 SSD), PSU, case.

Typical total: for 500 GB of private storage · Difficulty: 3/5 · Time: 2 hours

The maths: 2 TB of commercial cloud storage costs roughly $120 per year, forever. A Pi plus a 1 TB SSD costs about once. After two years it is cheaper — and after four you have saved a lot, with zero privacy questions.

Add Tailscale or WireGuard and you can reach your files from anywhere in the world as if you were at home.

🛡️ 4 · Block ads and trackers for the whole house

Pi-hole is a DNS server that answers "this is an ad server" with nothing. Every phone, TV, tablet and computer on your network instantly loads pages faster and stops leaking data — without installing anything on those devices.

What you need: Pi Zero 2 W or any spare Pi, PSU, 16 GB SD.

Typical total: · Difficulty: 2/5 · Time: 45 minutes

  • Typical result: 15–30 % of all DNS requests blocked, in the tens of thousands per day
  • Also blocks ads inside mobile apps, which browser extensions cannot
  • Optionally pair with Unbound for a completely private DNS resolver

Why it helps: faster browsing, less tracking, less malware, and you finally see how much of the modern web is advertising machinery.

📺 5 · Turn any TV into a smart media centre

Install LibreELEC (Kodi) or use VLC and your TV can play literally anything: MKV files, subtitles, network shares, USB drives, live TV with a tuner stick, and streaming add-ons. No "this app is not available in your country", no forced updates that break the remote, no ads.

What you need: Pi 4 · 2 GB or Pi 5, PSU, microSD, HDMI cable; optionally an IR remote or a phone app as the remote.

Typical total: · Difficulty: 1/5 · Time: 30 minutes

  • Hardware video decoding means smooth 1080p and good 4K on the Pi 5
  • CEC support: your TV remote controls Kodi
  • Pair it with the NAS project below and you have your own Netflix of files you own

Bonus: the same Pi can be a digital sign — display a dashboard, a shop menu, a timetable or a photo slideshow 24/7.

🕹️ 6 · Retro gaming console

RetroPie, Lakka or Batocera packages emulators for arcade machines, NES, SNES, Mega Drive, Game Boy, PlayStation 1, N64, Dreamcast, PSP and more — with a controller-friendly interface and box art, on your TV.

What you need: Pi 4 or Pi 5, PSU, microSD 32 GB+, 2 USB or Bluetooth gamepads, HDMI.

Typical total: with two controllers · Difficulty: 2/5 · Time: 1 hour

  • Pi 4 handles everything up to PS1, and most N64/Dreamcast titles
  • Pi 5 (with active cooler) is noticeably better at N64, Saturn and PSP
  • Use only games you legally own — ROMs of copyrighted titles are your responsibility

Why it helps: it is the cheapest, easiest way to learn how emulation, storage images and controllers work — and it is genuinely fun for a whole family.

🗄️ 7 · File, backup and document server

A Raspberry Pi with one or two USB/NVMe disks becomes a Network Attached Storage: shared folders for every device, automatic phone photo backup, Time Machine targets for Macs, and scheduled rsync copies so a dead drive never means lost photos.

What you need: Pi 5 · 4/8 GB, M.2 HAT+ with NVMe, powered USB hub or external PSU for spinning disks, case with fan.

Typical total: with 1 TB · Difficulty: 3/5 · Time: 2–3 hours

  • Samba/SMB shares that appear in Windows Explorer and macOS Finder
  • Nightly rsync mirror to a second disk = a real backup
  • Add Jellyfin for a proper streaming library with posters and subtitles

Honest warning: a Pi is not a RAID server for a business. For family photos and documents, with a second copy somewhere else, it is excellent.

🌐 8 · Website, API and development server

Run nginx, Node.js, Python/FastAPI, PostgreSQL or MySQL, Redis, and your own CI runner in Docker containers. Perfect for learning modern deployment, for staging sites before they go live, for internal tools, and for hosting a small personal site behind Cloudflare.

What you need: Pi 5 · 8 GB recommended, NVMe SSD for database performance, 27 W PSU, active cooling.

Typical total: · Difficulty: 3/5 · Time: an afternoon

  • Docker Compose stacks: web + database + reverse proxy with HTTPS
  • Git server (Gitea/Forgejo) with your own repositories
  • Scheduled jobs, webhooks, scrapers, monitoring dashboards (Grafana + Prometheus)
  • Minecraft or Valheim server for friends (Minecraft plays well with 4–8 players on a Pi 5)

Why it helps: you learn the exact skills cloud engineers are paid for, without a monthly hosting bill.

📷 9 · Cameras, security and monitoring

With the Camera Module 3 or the AI Camera, a Pi becomes a motion-detecting recorder that keeps footage on your own disk. Use Frigate with person/vehicle detection, or motionEyeOS for a simple multi-camera view.

What you need: Pi 5 (or Pi 4 for light use), Camera Module 3 $25 or AI Camera $70, ribbon cable, 64 GB+ storage, weatherproof enclosure for outdoor use.

Typical total: per camera · Difficulty: 3/5 · Time: 2 hours

  • Detects people only, so you do not get 400 alerts from a moving tree
  • Records 24/7 to disk with automatic retention rules
  • No monthly subscription, no footage leaving the house

Also used for: time-lapse of a construction site, plant growth monitoring, 3D-printed bird feeder cameras, and printing-progress cameras on 3D printers.

💼 10 · A working desktop for school or office

Browsing with hardware acceleration, LibreOffice for Word/Excel/PowerPoint files, PDF reading, email, printing, Zoom-class video calls (1080p with the Pi 5), note-taking, coding, and remote desktop into other machines. For most schoolwork and clerical work that is genuinely enough.

What you need: Pi 5 · 8 GB, PSU, NVMe SSD (big daily-life improvement over an SD card), case with fan, any monitor/keyboard/mouse.

Typical total: complete, or for the Pi 500 kit · Difficulty: 1/5 · Time: 20 minutes

  • Idle power 3 W instead of 60–150 W for a desktop tower
  • Silent, tiny, and cheap enough to keep a spare
  • Perfect for a shared family computer or a classroom of ten

Be realistic: heavy video editing, modern 3D games and Windows-only professional software are not what this is for.

🤖 11 · Electronics, robots and real-world control

This is the part a laptop simply cannot do. GPIO pins plus the Pico let you read sensors and drive things: motors, relays, solenoids, stepper drivers, servos, LED strips, pumps, buzzers and displays — with libraries already written for almost every component on sale.

What you need: Pico 2 $5 or Pi 5 with a breadboard kit; sensors and motors as your project requires.

Typical starter total: (Pico + breadboard + jumpers + LEDs + sensors) · Difficulty: 2/5 to start, 5/5 for serious robots

  • Starter builds: temperature logger, plant watering, ultrasonic parking sensor, servo cat feeder
  • Intermediate: line-following robot, CNC/3D-printer control, home energy meter reading
  • Serious: agricultural monitoring, machine retrofits, industrial data collection with a Compute Module

Why it helps: students who build these become the engineers companies fight over. Portfolios beat certificates.

🧠 12 · AI and computer vision at the edge

The Pi 5 plus the AI HAT+ or AI Camera runs neural networks locally: face and object detection, licence-plate reading, pose estimation, anomaly detection in factories, and small language models (via Ollama) for chat or document summarising — on about 10 watts, with data that never leaves the building.

What you need: Pi 5 · 8/16 GB, AI HAT+ (13 or 26 TOPS) or AI Camera, active cooling, NVMe storage.

Typical total: · Difficulty: 4/5 · Time: a weekend per project

  • Camera projects: counting people, reading meters, detecting safety gear, sorting objects
  • Language projects: local chat assistant, voice transcription with Whisper, OCR of documents
  • It is also the cheapest way to learn how model deployment actually works: quantisation, latency, memory, accuracy trade-offs

Reality check: this is edge AI, not GPT-scale training. But "small model, real device, real data" is exactly what most industrial AI jobs involve.

Be realistic

What a Raspberry Pi is not good at

  • Heavy gaming. No discrete GPU, no Windows game ecosystem. Only emulation and indie titles.
  • Professional video editing. 4K timeline editing will frustrate you.
  • Windows-only software. AutoCAD, Adobe CC, most accounting packages — forget it.
  • Business-critical RAID arrays. USB buses and single NVMe slots are not enterprise storage.
  • Anything needing a GPU for AI training. Inference yes, training no.
  • Cheap SD-card reliability. A cheap card will corrupt your system. Buy A1/A2 from a real brand.

Weekend wins

Six more things you can do in an afternoon

🖨️ Wi-Fi printer server

Share an old USB printer to every phone and laptop with CUPS. Cost: whatever Pi you have. Time: 30 min.

🌦️ Weather station

BME280 sensor + Pi or Pico: temperature, humidity, pressure, logged to a web dashboard. Cost: $20 extra.

🔒 WireGuard VPN

Access your home network and files securely from anywhere. Cost: $0 extra. Time: 45 min.

🎵 Whole-house audio

Snapcast or Mopidy multi-room streaming with old speakers and a cheap amp HAT.

📊 Network monitor

Uptime Kuma + Prometheus so you know the moment your internet or a server dies — with alerts.

🛰️ LoRa / long-range sensors

Send sensor data from a farm or remote building kilometres away without Wi-Fi or GSM fees.
