About this project

An independent guide, built by one person

This site exists for one reason: most Raspberry Pi information online is either marketing, outdated, or written for people who already know the answers. This guide is the opposite — plain language, real prices, honest limits, and step-by-step instructions that were written to be followed.

MA

Mohammed Al-Quwaizani

Author · Editor · Developer of this website

Mohammed designed, wrote and built every part of this guide: the structure of the seven sections, the model comparisons, the price tables and calculators, the virtual-machine lab, and the interface you are reading right now. Everything here is hand-written — no template, no page builder, no content farm.

The goal is practical: a person who has never touched a single-board computer should be able to read one page, decide what to buy, spend the right amount of money, and get something working the same day. And a person who does not want to spend anything yet should be able to start today, for free, using the virtual machine guide.

Methodology

How prices, specifications and commands are handled

Credibility comes from saying exactly where numbers come from. Here is the whole method.

  1. Board prices come from official list prices

    The per-model figures follow the manufacturer's published list price for each memory variant. They are the fairest comparable baseline, because reseller prices vary by country, stock and shipping.

  2. Kits and accessories are typical market ranges

    Third-party bundles differ too much for a single number, so they are shown as a range (e.g. $110–135) based on widely available bundles, not on one shop's promotion.

  3. Everything is stored in USD, then converted

    Prices are written once in US dollars and converted in your browser with approximate exchange rates, so the whole site can be read in a meaningful currency. Exchange rates move — treat non-USD figures as guidance, not quotes.

  1. Totals are complete, never just the board

    Every "total cost" in this guide includes what is genuinely required to make the project work: power supply, storage, cooling, cables. A price that ignores those is not a real price.

  2. Specifications follow the current generation

    Pi 5, Pi 4 Model B, Zero 2 W, Pico 2, Pi 400/500 and Compute Modules. Anything marked legacy is legacy hardware, kept because it is still useful second-hand.

  3. Commands are written to be pasted

    Shell examples are standard, widely used recipes, and where a platform behaves differently (for example, line continuation in PowerShell) the difference is called out explicitly rather than hidden.

⚠️

Always verify before buying. Prices, stock and taxes change constantly. Check the official approved reseller for your country on the day you order. Nothing on this site is an offer or a quote.

Editorial standards

Five rules the writing follows

1 No hype

If a Raspberry Pi cannot do something — heavy gaming, professional video editing, Windows-only software, business-grade reliability — it is stated plainly instead of being glossed over.

2 Numbers over adjectives

"2–3× faster", "3.5 W idle", "$168 complete setup" instead of "blazing fast" and "amazing value".

3 Costs are complete

Every budget includes the boring parts: the official power supply, the good memory card, the cooler, the cable you forgot.

4 Beginnings are free

Every section that requires spending money also explains how to try the same thing for nothing in a virtual machine.

5 Corrections are welcome

If a price, a specification or a command is wrong or has gone out of date, it gets fixed and noted in the changelog below.

6 Attribution matters

Tools and projects referenced here — Pi-hole, Home Assistant, Nextcloud, Frigate, Kodi and others — belong to their communities and are credited as such.
